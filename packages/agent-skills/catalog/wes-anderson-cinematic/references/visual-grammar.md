# Visual Grammar

Use this reference to turn the treatment into specific art-direction decisions. Choose the few rules that strengthen the user's concept; do not mechanically apply every rule.

## Composition

- Anchor the frame on a strong vertical or horizontal centerline.
- Use near-bilateral symmetry, centered one-point perspective, or deliberately balanced groups.
- Present rooms, façades, train compartments, counters, shelves, stages, and landscapes as readable tableaux.
- Use doors, arches, windows, cabinets, wallpaper panels, and furniture to subdivide the frame.
- Build three readable depth bands when useful: foreground frame, principal action plane, quiet background plane.
- Repeat props or figures rhythmically, usually in small odd-numbered groups.
- Leave controlled negative space around the focal subject.

Break the grid only when the story requires unease, absence, conflict, discovery, or a comic reveal. Make the break visible and purposeful.

## Camera and Lens Feel

- Prefer a frontal eye-level camera with minimal roll, tilt, or perspective distortion.
- Use a natural-to-slightly-wide perspective, roughly equivalent to 28–50 mm, according to space and subject scale.
- Keep lines straight and production geometry legible.
- Use moderate or deep focus so the designed environment participates in the image.
- Use exact overhead views for maps, food, letters, tools, luggage, evidence, or ritualized action.
- Use profile views for clean lateral movement.

Avoid a default language of extreme wide-angle distortion, dutch angles, floating drone moves, long-lens paparazzi compression, or constant shallow focus.

## Production Design

- Treat the set as a designed information system: every wall color, label, prop, textile, and piece of furniture should support character or story.
- Favor tactile painted wood, matte metal, worn leather, patterned paper, wool, cotton, ceramic, glass, cardboard, model-making materials, and carefully aged surfaces.
- Use restrained repetition, catalog-like organization, labeled compartments, maps, books, uniforms, luggage, instruments, stationery, and practical miniatures when relevant.
- Keep a consistent historical or invented era. Combine decades only with an explicit narrative reason.
- For products, keep the real package geometry and label hierarchy intact; let the set carry most of the treatment.

## Palette

Choose one palette family and one accent rather than using every signature color at once.

### Pastel hotel

- warm cream;
- powder pink;
- faded coral;
- dusty rose;
- burgundy accent;
- walnut or oxblood grounding.

### Coastal expedition

- desaturated sky blue;
- turquoise;
- seafoam;
- sand;
- mustard accent;
- navy grounding.

### Autumn institution

- parchment;
- camel;
- tobacco brown;
- olive;
- muted orange;
- brick-red accent.

### Clean commercial

- off-white or pale neutral field;
- one product-derived dominant color;
- one contrasting accent;
- dark neutral for text and outline.

Describe relative color relationships when exact brand colors are unavailable. If exact colors are supplied, preserve them.

## Lighting and Finish

- Use soft frontal daylight, diffused window light, even theatrical key light, or warm practical pools with readable fill.
- Keep contrast moderate and faces legible.
- Preserve matte material response and gentle filmic roll-off.
- Use subtle fine grain only when it supports the requested medium.
- Keep shadows graphic but not crushed.

Avoid default teal-orange grading, wet glossy surfaces, heavy bloom, volumetric haze, excessive lens flare, and muddy sepia filters.

## People and Performance

- Block individuals square to camera or in clean profile.
- Use composed posture, direct gaze, restrained expressions, precise hand positions, and small deliberate gestures.
- Arrange ensembles by height, costume color, role, or narrative allegiance.
- Give action a visible start and finish: enter, stop, look, place, reveal, exit.
- Let emotion appear through timing, stillness, spatial separation, or one small asymmetry instead of melodramatic posing.

## Graphics and Typography

- Use centered chapter cards, object labels, room numbers, itinerary fragments, maps, or catalog captions only when narratively useful.
- Favor bookish serif, geometric sans, or tidy hand-lettered forms consistent with the scene's era.
- Limit visible copy. Preserve user-supplied wording and case exactly.
- Specify a clean blank area for text when the model is likely to render it poorly, then add typography downstream.

## Motion Grammar

### Locked tableau

Hold the camera still while subjects enter, align, perform one action, and settle into a final composition.

### Lateral track

Move parallel to the action at a constant measured speed. Keep subject scale and horizon stable. Reveal adjacent rooms, compartments, or characters in sequence.

### Centered push-in

Start on a balanced wide or medium tableau. Move directly toward the central subject without orbiting. End on a precise reaction, object, or clue.

### Whip-pan bridge

Start from a stable frame, pan rapidly on a motivated cue, and land on another stable frontal composition. Specify direction and landing subject.

### Overhead insert

Cut to an exact top-down view of an action or organized object layout. Keep hands, props, and orientation continuous with adjacent shots.

### Match cut

Align shape, color, position, or gesture between the outgoing and incoming frame. Name the matched element explicitly.

## Treatment Variants

### Live-action storybook

Keep people, materials, and locations believable. Achieve the treatment through staging, architecture, palette, costume coordination, and disciplined camera placement.

### Miniature theatre

Declare scale, material system, edge treatment, and animation method. Use visible handcrafted detail, controlled depth, and miniature lighting. Choose clay, felt, paper, painted wood, or model-board as a coherent system instead of mixing all craft media.

### Retro editorial

Simplify the background, strengthen product or subject silhouette, preserve packaging and branding, and use a catalog-like composition with one playful prop relationship. Keep commercial readability ahead of cinematic decoration.
