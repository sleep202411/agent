# Cinematic Realism

Preserve the creator's subject, project facts, approved references, identity, exact copy, delivery constraints, and safety policy. Apply only the visual direction required by the requested artifact.

## Visual Grammar

- Build shots from motivated camera placement, plausible lenses, practical or naturally motivated light, and production-ready blocking.
- Preserve real material response: skin texture, fabric weight, surface wear, atmospheric depth, and contact shadows.
- Prefer restrained color science, readable exposure, and specific environmental detail over synthetic spectacle.
- Direct performances through observable gaze, posture, breath, weight shift, and interaction with the set.

## Prompt Authoring

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:prompt:start -->

Use grounded live-action film language: motivated camera and lens choices, physically credible lighting, natural material response, production-readable blocking, restrained color science, and subtle human performance. Avoid plastic skin, weightless motion, excessive bloom, generic teal-orange grading, and impossible depth of field.

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:prompt:end -->

## Image Generation

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:image:start -->

Render as a grounded live-action film frame with plausible lens perspective, motivated practical light, natural skin and fabric texture, material wear, atmospheric depth, contact shadows, restrained highlight roll-off, and an unforced cinematic composition. Keep faces and bodies anatomically credible; avoid glossy synthetic polish.

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:image:end -->

## Video Generation

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:video:start -->

Stage motion with believable weight, inertia, eyelines, foot contact, cloth response, and environmental interaction. Use motivated camera movement, stable spatial continuity, natural performance beats, coherent light through the shot, and a composed end frame. Avoid floaty bodies, morphing anatomy, or gratuitous camera motion.

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:video:end -->

## Boundaries

- Do not override explicit creator requirements, approved references, permissions, safety policy, model capability, or tool contracts.
- Do not invent assets, approvals, project facts, dialogue, inspected evidence, or completed generation results.
- Do not add executable tools, third-party credentials, or direct provider calls.
- Use original characters and settings; do not recreate recognizable protected frames the creator did not provide.
