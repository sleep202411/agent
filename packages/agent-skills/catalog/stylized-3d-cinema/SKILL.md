# Stylized 3D Cinema

Preserve the creator's subject, project facts, approved references, identity, exact copy, delivery constraints, and safety policy. Apply only the visual direction required by the requested artifact.

## Visual Grammar

- Design silhouettes and proportions deliberately; stylization must remain consistent across characters, props, and architecture.
- Use tactile, simplified materials with readable roughness, subsurface response, and controlled detail density.
- Shape scenes with graphic key light, clear value grouping, atmospheric depth, and cinematic lens choices.
- Keep motion appealing through weight, arcs, anticipation, follow-through, and stable character volume.

## Prompt Authoring

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:prompt:start -->

Use feature-animation 3D language with intentionally designed proportions, coherent silhouette families, tactile simplified materials, graphic value grouping, cinematic lenses, atmospheric depth, and expressive but physically readable performance. Avoid default game-engine gloss, noisy micro-detail, inconsistent stylization, and toy-like weightlessness.

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:prompt:end -->

## Image Generation

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:image:start -->

Render as a polished stylized 3D film frame with designed proportions, strong readable silhouettes, tactile materials, controlled surface detail, graphic key lighting, soft atmospheric depth, cinematic composition, and appealing facial expression. Avoid generic glossy plastic, flat viewport lighting, or photoreal skin pasted onto stylized forms.

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:image:end -->

## Video Generation

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:video:start -->

Animate with clear anticipation, appealing arcs, believable weight, squash and stretch only where the design supports it, stable volume, coherent materials, motivated cinematic camera movement, and a strong final pose. Avoid rubbery deformation, sliding feet, or uncontrolled camera orbiting.

<!-- fizzdragon:policy:video:end -->

## Boundaries

- Do not override explicit creator requirements, approved references, permissions, safety policy, model capability, or tool contracts.
- Do not invent assets, approvals, project facts, dialogue, inspected evidence, or completed generation results.
- Do not add executable tools, third-party credentials, or direct provider calls.
- Use original designs; do not imitate a protected studio character or franchise.
